What to Include on a Professional Invoice

  • Your business name, logo, and contact information (address, email, phone)
  • Client name, company, and billing address
  • Unique invoice number (sequential, e.g. INV-2024-001)
  • Invoice date and payment due date
  • Itemized list of products/services with description, quantity, rate, and amount
  • Subtotal before tax
  • Sales tax amount and rate (if applicable)
  • Total amount due
  • Payment terms (e.g. "Net 30", "Due on receipt")
  • Accepted payment methods
  • Notes or additional terms

US Tax Requirements for Invoices

US businesses don't charge VAT but may charge sales tax depending on state and type of product/service. Service businesses are often exempt in many states. Include your EIN on invoices to look professional, especially regarding the 1099 threshold ($600). Keep in mind there are state-by-state variations.

How to Set Payment Terms That Get You Paid

Common terms include Net 7, Net 15, Net 30, and Due on receipt. Shorter terms often work better for freelancers. Consider late payment fees (e.g., 1.5% monthly) and deposit requirements for larger projects. Ensure clear due dates instead of relying on "Net 30" ambiguity, and state payment methods explicitly.

Common Invoice Mistakes That Delay Payment

  1. Missing or wrong contact details — client can't reach you for questions
  2. No invoice number — hard to track and reference
  3. Vague line item descriptions — "consulting" vs "Brand strategy session, 4 hours"
  4. Missing due date — "payment within 30 days" of what date?
  5. No payment method instructions — client doesn't know how to pay
  6. Sending PDF via email without follow-up — lost in inbox
  7. Not including your EIN when working with US businesses

Invoice Best Practices

Send invoices immediately after completing work, use a consistent numbering system, and follow up 3 days before the due date. You can use WhatsApp for faster delivery to mobile-first clients. Always keep a copy of every invoice sent, and use a professional PDF format instead of photos or screenshots.
